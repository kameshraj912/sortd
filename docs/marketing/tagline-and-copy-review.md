Written 27 Sep 2026, updated same day with eight more candidates and two subtitles.
Reviews the current slogan, sixteen candidates, two App Store subtitles, the Welcome screen
and the founder's note against five target readers. One file, no edits made anywhere else.

not confirm either against the live page myself, so I'm not counting them in the pattern
below. [Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/) is a counter-example, not a good one: its

what "too long" looks like.

**What the short, loved ones share:**
1. **Under seven words** in every verified example except Monzo (10, but reads as one
   breath) and MoneyCoach (10, and it's the weakest of the set — a device list, not a
   benefit).
2. **A verb first**, or a verb right after "the": Spend, Simplify, Send, Get. Sortd's
   current line already does this ("Tap to pay").
3. **The benefit in a feeling word**, not a feature word: "life", "truth", "love", "less
   stress" — never "seamless", "optimise", "insights".
4. **No jargon, no adjective doing the work.** Copilot's "beautifully organized" is the one
   weak spot in an otherwise strong set — it's an adjective standing in for a benefit.

Sortd's current line, "Tap to pay. Sortd writes it down.", already matches the pattern:
six words, a verb first, the benefit stated as a plain action, no adjective. That matters
for Part 2.

## Part 2 — Five people react

Personas built from Sortd's stated audience (Australia and Singapore, phone-tap payers,
some living between two countries). Not real people; not quotes from anyone.

- **P1 — Mia, 20, Melbourne student.** Taps for everything, cash almost never touches her
  hands. Skims fast, likes dry jokes, doesn't care about bank trauma because she's never
  had a "real" bank relationship to distrust.
- **P2 — Nurul, 29, Singapore nurse.** Rotating shifts. Reads things half-asleep. Wants the
  plainest possible sentence; no patience for cleverness at 6am.
- **P3 — Daniel, 34, Sydney ⇄ Singapore for work.** Cards in two countries, two currencies.
  Wants to trust an app with financial info across borders; suspicious of anything vague
  about where data goes.
- **P4 — Grace, 45, tried a bank-linking app and quit over the password screen.** The most
  guarded reader here. Anything that doesn't say "no bank login" out loud gets a raised
  eyebrow until she reads the fine print.
- **P5 — Ben, 23, has never budgeted, finds money apps preachy.** Allergic to being lectured.
  Any hint of "you should" or "get good with money" makes him close the app.

### Recommendation

**Headline — keep the current slogan: "Tap to pay. Sortd writes it down."** It won outright
across all seventeen candidates (22/25), it's already in `brand-voice.md` as an approved
CTA, it matches every pattern in Part 1 (verb first, plain action, no adjective), it splits
cleanest into caption cards on mute, and it's the clearest opener for a stranger who's never
heard of Sortd. Since the brief for this round said the winner replaces the line everywhere,
the result is: **don't change it anywhere** — Welcome screen, About, site and listing already
carry it or a close variant.

Keep two in reserve, not as the headline: **H** ("Where your money went. No bank login.") for
anywhere the pitch is aimed at someone already burned by a bank-linking app, and **N**
("Sortd remembers what you paid. (You don't have to.)") specifically for reel captions,
since its bracket is built exactly like the "aside" pattern in `brand-voice.md`.

**Subtitle — switch to "Spending that logs itself"** (25 characters, counted above, under
Apple's 30-char limit). It scored higher than the subtitle currently in
`docs/AppStoreListing.md` (21/25 vs 19/25) on the identical test, reads a half-beat faster
for the two personas who read it tired or worried, and carries no compliance risk (unlike
"Apple Pay, logged by itself", which tested just as well but can't ship — see above). This
is a change from my first pass; the extra testing this round moved the recommendation.

### Onboarding lines that failed with two or more personas

Checked: the Welcome screen (`SetupPages.swift`, `SetupCopy`) and founder-note Version A.
Most of it holds up — the founder note's "I built this instead" line and "No bank login,
no account" repetition tested clean with all five. Two lines did not:

1. **"Bills, seen coming"** (Welcome screen, feature row title,
   `OnboardingView.swift` line 663). Mia and Nurul both stumbled on the phrase itself before
   reading the detail line under it — "seen coming" reads like a typo or a riddle on first
   pass, not a benefit.
   **Plainer rewrite:** *"Bills before they hit"* — same four beats, no ambiguity, pairs
   cleanly with the existing detail line "Know what's due before it's charged."

2. **"Private by design"** (Welcome screen, feature row title, same file, line 664). Mia
   read it as filler ("sounds like it's from a pitch deck"), and Ben flagged it as exactly
   the kind of buzzword that makes an app sound like it's trying to sell him something —
   ironic, since the actual claim underneath ("No bank login. Stays on your iPhone.") is
   the strongest, plainest thing Sortd can say.
   **Plainer rewrite:** *"No bank login"* — drop the marketing-flavoured title entirely and
   let the existing detail line stand on its own: "No bank login. Stays on your iPhone."
