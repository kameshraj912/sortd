# Founder's note (in-app)

For a screen that shows once: either right after the first Apple Pay purchase logs
itself, or on first launch. Three versions below, one recommended.

## Research: what makes these work

I could not find the verbatim on-screen text for most of these notes — they live
inside app binaries and TestFlight builds, not on the open web, so search engines
don't index them. What I could verify, with sources:

- **Overcast (Marco Arment).** Widely singled out for onboarding that reads like one
  person talking to you, not a company: "friendly and well-written subtitles
  throughout the app that remain helpful without being patronising or annoying."
  [Thoughts on Overcast — alexwlchan.net](https://alexwlchan.net/2014/overcast/)
  (read 27 Sep 2026). No verbatim founder-note text found; the pattern is tone, not a
  single quoted note.
- **Superhuman (Rahul Vohra).** Not an in-app note, but the same idea taken further:
  every early user got a real 1-on-1 onboarding call with a founder or team member,
  which is why people felt someone real was on the other end.
  [Superhuman's Onboarding Playbook — First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/superhuman-onboarding-playbook/)
  (read 27 Sep 2026).
- **Apollo (Christian Selig).** When Apollo shut down, Selig's own sign-off — short,
  first person, no corporate voice — is the clearest real example of "one true reason,
  signed by a real name" landing hard: "thank you so much for all the years of fun, it
  was the honor of a lifetime building the app for all of you."
  [RevenueCat: Apollo's Rise & Shutdown, a conversation with Christian Selig](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/christian-selig-apollo-sub-club-podcast/)
  (read 27 Sep 2026); goodbye post referenced on
  [Selig's Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/@christianselig/110635082738846427).
- **The Browser Company / Arc (Josh Miller).** Miller writes long personal letters to
  users that own mistakes plainly ("we should have paused Arc a year earlier," "we
  dragged our feet") rather than spin them.
  [Stratechery: An Interview with Ryan Jones... ](https://stratechery.com/2025/an-interview-with-ryan-jones-about-flighty-and-building-apps-in-2025/)
  and coverage of the letter at
  [Message from Josh Miller — Ten Forums](https://www.tenforums.com/windows-10-news/216310-message-josh-miller-ceo-browser-company-arc-more.html)
  (read 27 Sep 2026).
- **Halide, Bear, Flighty, Things, Copilot Money, YNAB, Craft, Notion.** Searched for
  each by name plus "founder note" / "welcome note" / "onboarding message". Mobbin and
  design-inspiration sites confirm these apps *have* welcome or onboarding screens, but
  none of the search results surfaced the actual sentences shown on those screens.
  **Not verified** — I'm not quoting these, since I can't confirm the wording. If Raj
  has screenshots or remembers the actual text, worth a second pass.

**What the confirmed examples have in common, and what I'm using:**
1. One true reason the app exists ("I got tired of X"), not a mission statement.
2. One small ask — reply, tell me what's broken — not "rate us" or "invite friends".
3. A real name, not "The Sortd Team".
4. Short. Every strong example above reads in under 10 seconds.
5. No marketing words. Selig's goodbye and Miller's letters both read like texts to a
   friend, including admitting what's still rough.

## Where it lands best

Three placements, ranked, each backed by something I could actually confirm:

1. **Best: right after the aha moment (a purchase just logged itself).** Nielsen
   Norman Group's onboarding research argues against upfront messaging on principle:
   "Users rarely download an app for no reason; therefore, lengthy promotional
   onboarding will likely be skipped," and instead favours help "while the user is in
   the app," timed to when a person has just done the thing the app is for. Appcues'
   pattern guide says the same from the growth side: "Messages should be timed by
   behavior, not by schedule, so help arrives exactly when a user needs it." A note
   that shows right after the first tap logs itself is proof-then-message, not
   message-then-hope. [NN/g: Mobile-App Onboarding](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-app-onboarding/) ·
   [Appcues: Onboarding UX patterns](https://www.appcues.com/blog/user-onboarding-ui-ux-patterns)
   (both read 27 Sep 2026).
2. **Second: after setup, before Home.** Still contextual — the person just finished
   the one manual step (the Shortcuts automation) — so the note can thank them for
   getting through the fiddly part, not for downloading. Weaker than option 1 only
   because nothing has happened yet that proves the app works; it's a promise, not a
   result.
3. **Third, and the one I'd avoid: first launch, before anything.** This is the
   placement NN/g specifically warns is where lengthy or promotional content gets
   skipped, since the person hasn't yet formed a reason to read it. Growth.Design's own
   teardown of HEY's onboarding shows the same trap in a different app: long,
   feature-heavy explainer screens before the person has done anything. [Growth.Design:
   Is HEY Email Worth It?](https://growth.design/case-studies/hey-user-onboarding)
   (read 27 Sep 2026). If Raj wants first-launch anyway (to set expectations before
   Gmail/camera permissions come up), Version B below is written for that slot
   specifically, kept as short as the other two for the same reason.

## Claims used (checked against this worktree, 27 Sep 2026)

- "No bank login, no account, nothing leaves your phone" — matches
  `docs/AppStoreListing.md` promotional text line 21 and Description line 27.
- "You tap, Sortd writes it down" — matches Description line 25.
- Feedback opens the reader's own Mail app addressed to `support@sortd.page` — this is
  a real, already-built path: `Spend/Views/Settings/HelpFeedbackSettingsView.swift`
  (`supportEmail = "support@sortd.page"`, builds a `mailto:` URL). Same address is used
  site-wide (`site/support.html`, `site/privacy.html`).
- I did **not** claim the app catches every Apple Pay tap (it can miss; Apple's Wallet
  trigger can time out) and I did **not** say anything about backup or data safety if
  the phone is lost — neither claim is needed for this note, so I left both out rather
  than risk overstating either one.
- Not signing "Raj", not naming any job, university or employer — per the brief.

## Version A — after the aha moment (first purchase just logged itself)

**Title:** It worked.

> Thanks for trying Sortd. That tap you just made — logged itself. That's the whole
> idea.
>
> No bank login, no account, nothing leaves your phone.
>
> I'm one person who got tired of typing every coffee into a spreadsheet that died
> every February, so I built this. If something looks off, or you want to say hi, I
> read every reply myself.

**— Kameshraj**
*Made in Melbourne and Singapore*

**Buttons:** `Keep going` (dismiss) · `Tell Kameshraj` (opens Mail to support@sortd.page)

Body word count: 58 words.

## Recommendation

**Use Version A**, shown right after the first purchase logs itself. That's the
placement the evidence in "Where it lands best" ranks first: the reader just watched
the one true reason happen on screen, so "I got tired of typing every coffee into a
spreadsheet" reads as fact, not pitch, and the thank-you lands on something that just
happened rather than something hoped for. Version B is the one to use instead only if
Raj wants the note to set expectations before Gmail or camera permissions ever come up
— written for first launch specifically, same length, same tone. Version C is the
fallback if there's only room for a small card, not a full screen, in either spot.
