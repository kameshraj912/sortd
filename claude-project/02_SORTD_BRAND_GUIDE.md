## 1. What Sortd is

**One line:** Your spending, logged by itself.

**Longer:** Sortd is an iPhone app that writes down your spending for you. Tap to pay with Apple Pay and it logs the shop, amount and card. It also reads receipts from Gmail and paper receipts from the camera, finds your subscriptions, and keeps it all in one list. Everything stays on your iPhone.

**The enemy:** typing purchases in by hand. Nobody does it, so every budget dies by February.

**Who it's for:** people who pay with their phone and have never kept a budget going past week three. Students and young workers in Australia and Singapore first — especially people with cards in two countries (that's Raj).

**Why it's credible:** built by one person who needed it. Australian card and a Singapore card, so multi-currency had to work.

## 3. Voice

### The character
Sortd talks like a friend who is smug, very online, and slightly too honest — and then actually helps you. It knows it's an ad. It says so. It roasts your habits the way a mate does: because it's on your side.

Two references to channel (never name them in posts):
- **The phone-skin brand energy** — roast the customer, roast yourself, make the product the punchline's payoff.
- **The fourth-wall hero energy** — self-aware, meta about marketing, breaks format on purpose.

### Five moves
1. **Say the quiet part.** "You were never going to type in that $5.50 coffee." Name the thing everyone does and nobody admits.
2. **Parenthetical aside.** The signature. Fact first, jab in brackets. "Sortd spots anything that repeats. (You have at least one. Everyone does.)"
3. **Self-own.** Joke about Sortd being tiny and solo. "We don't have a server. Or an office. Or a second employee."
4. **Break the fourth wall.** "This is the part of the ad where we say it's private. It is, though."
5. **Deadpan flat ending.** Short last line that undercuts. "That's it. That's the routine."

### Punch at / never punch at
| Fair game | Off limits |
|---|---|
| Takeaway ("It was takeaway. It's always takeaway.") | Debt, being broke, can't pay rent |
| Forgotten subscriptions | Anyone's income or job |
| Dead spreadsheets, abandoned budget apps (generic) | Named banks, named competitors, named people |
| Sortd itself, Raj (the solo maker) | Gambling, BNPL users, crypto losses |
| Kmart runs, 2 am online shopping | Bodies, gender, race, religion, mental health |
| Marketing clichés ("revolutionary", "AI-powered") | Real financial hardship |

Rule of thumb: **roast the habit, rescue the person.** The joke is never on someone for having less money.

### Words
- **Use:** logged, sorted, by itself, one list, stays on your iPhone, tap, bills, takeaway, spending.
- **Avoid:** revolutionary, seamless, empower, leverage, unlock, journey, game-changer, "AI-powered" (say what it does instead), financial freedom, wealth.
- Spelling: Australian/British (colour, organise, cheque). Numbers as digits. "$5.50", not "five dollars fifty".
- Name is always **Sortd** (capital S in text), **sortd** only in the lowercase wordmark. Never "Sorted", "SORTD" in body text, or "Sortd app".

### Spicy vs Mild
Every piece gets two versions.
- **Spicy:** full roast, fourth wall, weird format. For TikTok, Reels, X, Threads.
- **Mild:** same idea, one light jab, clear benefit. For LinkedIn, App Store text, emails, anything Raj's supervisor or a bank partner might read.

### The three lines (set 2 Oct 2026)
- Headline, says what it does: "Tap to pay. Sortd writes it down."
- Sign-off (end cards, footers, last line of a post, end of setup): "Consider it Sortd."
- After a tap logs (first-purchase card, a Reel's reveal): "You've been Sortd."
- Always spelt Sortd, never "sorted". Never "Get Sortd" (a registered Australian trade mark).

### Examples from the live site (the benchmark)
- "Tap to pay. Sortd writes it down."
- "Most budgets die by February."
- "We're not building a shrine to your Kmart runs."
- "Pay once. Never hear from us about it again."
- "Hackers would be very disappointed."

- "You printed a website for an app that scans paper receipts. Bold." (print easter egg)

2. **The phone** — real app screenshots in a thin black iPhone frame. Show light or dark to match the post.
3. **The notification** — the "Logged $5.50 at Seven Seeds Coffee · Eating Out" banner. The product in one frame.
4. **The four bars** — under headlines, as dividers, as a loading bar in video.
5. **Dark CTA panel** — near-black card with soft orange/violet/green glows behind, white text.

### Motion
Quick and springy (overshoot on arrivals), 0.3–0.9 s. The 3-beat story: **You pay → Sortd logs it → It's in your list.** Respect reduced-motion: every video must make sense muted and with captions.

Real screenshots only (`site/img/*-light.jpg` / `*-dark.jpg`: home, activity, cards, card, insights, recurring, gmail, setup). Sample data only — never show a real person's purchases. No stock photos of people holding cash. No Apple logo.

## 7. What we borrow from the references (and where they went wrong)

Researched 20 Sep 2026. Agency blogs and trade press are weaker sources; official pages are marked.

**From the phone-skin brand:** the voice *is* the product. Snarky replies get screenshotted and reused as ads. Product demo comes second to the headline. Hooks are short and provocative.
**Where it broke:** in 2024 a tweet mocked a customer's surname — big backlash, public apology, US$10,000 paid. In 2025 it blamed users for a product fault, then had to apologise and replace for free. Lesson for us: **never roast a real person's name, looks or complaint. When something breaks, drop the jokes and fix it.** (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dbrand)

**From the fourth-wall hero campaigns:** hijack a format — fake PSA, fake romance ad, fake legal disclaimer, fake film teaser that pivots to the product. Seasonal hooks. One consistent character. Put the fine print *inside* the joke. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_for_Deadpool_(film))

**From fintech humour (official — monzo.com/tone-of-voice):** people should be "part of the joke, not the target of it". Money often brings up shame, so no humour in support replies or bad news. Up Bank (AU) treats customers "like a mate" and grows by word of mouth.

**From unhinged social brands:** move fast (idea to post in about a week), but keep one person who judges the line. When the mood turns, go quiet fast.

### Format ideas this gives us
- **Fake PSA:** "This is a public service announcement. Your gym membership is still charging you."
- **Fake legal disclaimer:** "Sortd is not a financial adviser. Sortd is a very judgemental list."
- **Fake teaser → pivot:** moody film-trailer cut… it's a coffee being logged.
- **Valentine's hijack:** "Something that remembers everything you bought. Romantic, or a threat?"
- **End-of-financial-year (AU, 30 June) / Great Singapore Sale:** seasonal roasts.

## 8. Rules from Apple and the regulators

Not legal advice. Get a lawyer to check before paid ads or launch.

### Apple (official: developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines, apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html, developer.apple.com/apple-pay/marketing)
- **No App Store badge while in TestFlight.** Pre-order badge only if a pre-order is set up; swap to "Download on the App Store" at launch. One badge per layout, below the main message, not animated or tilted, min 40 px tall.
- Write **iPhone** — never "IPhone", never all caps, never "iPhones" or "iPhone's" (say "iPhone models"). Say "Sortd Money for iPhone".
- **Don't use the Apple Pay mark/logo.** Sortd doesn't take Apple Pay payments. Plain text like "logs your Apple Pay taps" is fine.
- No Apple logo. Nothing that suggests Apple endorses Sortd.
- **Device frames:** Apple wants its own bezels, unmodified — no tilting, cropping, shadows, reflections, or things popping out of the screen. Copy sits beside the phone, not on it. The screen must show the real app. (Note: the website uses a hand-drawn CSS iPhone frame. Fine on our own site, but for ads and the App Store use Apple's official bezels.)

### Australia
- **ACCC:** claims must be true and backed. Obvious exaggeration ("the world's most judgemental list") is fine. Anything that sounds like a fact must be one.
- **Free trials and subscriptions are an ACCC priority (2025–27).** Any "14 days free" must say, just as clearly, what happens after: "then US$29.99 for the first year, then US$49.99 a year. Cancel any time in the App Store." Fine print can't contradict the headline.
- **AANA Code:** ads must look like ads. Paid or gifted creator posts need a clear **#ad** (not just "#sp" or "collab").
- **ASIC:** budgeting tips like "cook lunch at home" are unlikely to be financial product advice. Don't recommend or compare bank accounts, cards, BNPL, super, crypto or investments.

### Singapore
- **ASAS code:** legal, decent, honest, truthful. Paid content marked **#ad**, visible before "more".
- **CCCS price rules:** no hidden extras, honest "was/now" prices, honest "free".
- **MAS** digital ad rules apply to MAS-regulated firms. Only matters if Sortd partners with a bank.

## 10. Beta and launch playbook

1. **Now (beta):** TestFlight takes up to 10,000 external testers through a public link you can post. The first external build needs Beta App Review. CTA: "Join the beta" → link.
2. **Build in public:** Raj's real story (two countries, one person, no server). Post progress, bugs, wins. Reddit, X, Threads, Indie Hackers — a founder story beats polish.
3. **Short demos:** treat each 10–20 s video as a cheap test. Double down on what works. Only pay to boost what already did well without paying.
4. **About 3–4 weeks before launch:** open the wider beta, set up a pre-order with manual release. Pre-order badge allowed from here.
5. **Launch week:** App Store screenshots and text first (most downloads come from App Store search). Then video. Paid ads last.

Sources: developer.apple.com/testflight; indie launch advice from vendor blogs (kickstart.tools, screenfast.app) — treat as rough guidance.
