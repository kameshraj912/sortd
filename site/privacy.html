Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 September 2026

What Sortd stores, and where

Everything Sortd stores is kept on your iPhone, inside the app's own storage:

  • Purchases — merchant, amount, currency, date, category, card, and any note you add. These are kept in an on-device database (Apple's SwiftData).
  • Cards — the name you give each card and its last 4 digits. Sortd never asks for or stores full card numbers, and never asks for bank logins or passwords.
  • Settings — such as your home currency, budgets, and the categories Sortd has learned from your changes.
  • Your setup answers — what you want from Sortd, how you usually pay, how you feel about your spending, when you'd like a check-in, and how often you spend abroad. See Setup questions.
  • A summary for your widgets — totals, recent purchases and bills due, so Sortd's widgets can show them. It sits in a folder on your iPhone that only Sortd and its widgets can open.
  • Exchange rates — daily rates Sortd has downloaded, so it doesn't fetch them again.
  • Connected Gmail addresses — the address and when it last synced, if you connect Gmail.

We don't run a server, so none of this is sent to us. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, Sortd's data is included in that backup like other app data, under Apple's terms.

How we protect your data

Some of what Sortd handles is sensitive: your purchases, and, if you connect it, your Gmail. This is how it is kept safe.

  • Kept on your iPhone only: Sortd has no server and no Sortd account. Your purchases, cards, settings and Gmail data never reach us or any other company, so we hold no copy for anyone to leak. A backup or spreadsheet file only exists if you choose to share one, and Sortd deletes its own copy afterwards.
  • Encrypted on your iPhone: Sortd's storage is protected by iOS Data Protection. It is encrypted, and after your iPhone restarts it can't be read until you unlock the phone with your passcode or Face ID. Other apps can't read it.
  • Google sign-in key in the Keychain: the Google refresh token is stored in the iPhone Keychain, which iOS encrypts. It is set to stay on this iPhone only: it is not synced to iCloud Keychain and does not move to another device. Short-lived access keys are kept only in memory and are never written to storage.
  • Secure sign-in: you sign in to Google in Apple's secure sign-in window, using OAuth 2.0 with PKCE. Sortd never sees your Google password, and no secret key is built into the app.
  • Encrypted in transit: every connection Sortd makes, to Google and to frankfurter.dev, uses HTTPS (TLS). Sortd makes no unencrypted connections.
  • Least access: Sortd asks Google only for read-only Gmail access. It can't send, change or delete email. It searches only for receipts and bank alerts.
  • Emails are not stored: an email's text is held in memory only while Sortd reads the purchase from it, then discarded. Sortd keeps the purchase details and the email's ID, not the email.
  • No people, no third parties: no one at Sortd can see your data, because it never leaves your iPhone. Sortd has no analytics, ads, crash-reporting or tracking tools, and does not share or sell data.
  • Removing access: Disconnect and Delete All Data ask Google to cancel Sortd's access and delete the key from the Keychain. You can also remove Sortd at any time in your Google Account's security settings.

If you find a security problem in Sortd, please email support@sortd.page. We will look into it and fix it as fast as we can.

Logging Apple Pay taps

Sortd does not read your Apple Wallet by itself. If you choose, you create an automation in Apple's Shortcuts app on your iPhone. When you pay with Apple Pay, that automation passes the transaction (merchant, amount and card name) to Sortd's "Log Wallet Tap" or "Log Purchase" action. This happens on your iPhone. Sortd keeps a copy of the last tap it received, on your iPhone, so you can check the setup. You can turn the automation off or delete it in the Shortcuts app at any time.

Card digits: you can give Sortd the last 4 digits of each card and of its Apple Pay number (the Device Account Number). Sortd uses them only to match purchases to the right card. They stay on your iPhone. Sortd never asks for or stores a full card number.

Setup questions

When you first set up Sortd, it asks a few short questions: what you want from it, how you usually pay, how you feel about your spending, when you'd like a check-in, and how often you spend abroad. You can skip them.

  • Where they're kept: on your iPhone only, in Sortd's own storage. They are never sent to us or anyone else, and they aren't put in backup files.
  • What they're used for: choosing which setup steps to show you, which Sortd Pro features to show first, and when your check-in comes. Nothing else.
  • Changing them: Settings › Help & Feedback › Run Setup Again. You can change just the check-in time in Settings › Bills & Reminders. Running setup again keeps your purchases and cards.
  • Deleting them: Delete All Data, or deleting the app, removes them.

Receipt scanning (optional)

If you tap "Scan Receipt", Sortd asks for camera access, or lets you pick one photo. It reads the text on the receipt on your iPhone using Apple's built-in text recognition and, on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model. The photo is not saved by Sortd and is not sent anywhere. Sortd keeps only what you confirm on the review screen: the shop, amount, currency, date and category. On iPhones without Apple Intelligence, the text is read with simple rules instead.

Typing a purchase

When you add a purchase, you can type it the way you'd say it, like "coffee 5.50 yesterday". On iPhones with Apple Intelligence turned on, Sortd uses Apple's on-device model to read that line. It runs on your iPhone, and the text is not sent to us, to Apple or to anyone else.

Sortd checks what the model gives back. For example, an amount you typed as a number always wins, and a currency is only used if your line mentions it. The result only fills in the Add form. Nothing is saved until you check it and tap save. Sortd doesn't keep the line you typed. On iPhones without Apple Intelligence, or if you turn it off, the line is read with simple rules instead.

Importing a statement

If you choose to import a bank statement (a CSV or PDF file, or a screenshot of your bank app), Sortd reads it on your iPhone and shows you what it found. Only the purchases you tick are saved. The file is not uploaded and Sortd doesn't keep a copy of it.

Gmail (optional)

If you tap "Connect Gmail", you sign in to Google in Apple's secure sign-in window. Sortd never sees your Google password.

  • What Sortd asks Google for: read-only Gmail access (gmail.readonly), plus your email address so Sortd can show which account is connected. Sortd cannot send, delete, move or change your email.
  • What it reads: Sortd searches only for receipts and bank alerts (for example, emails Gmail files under Purchases, or with subjects like "receipt", "invoice" or "order confirmation"). The first sync looks back 120 days. Other email is not opened.
  • Where it reads them: on your iPhone. Emails go straight from Google's Gmail API to your iPhone. Nothing is sent anywhere else, and no copy is kept on any server.
  • What it keeps: the purchase details it finds (merchant, amount, currency, date, and the card's last 4 digits if the email shows them) and each email's ID, so it doesn't read the same email twice. It does not keep a copy of the emails.
  • On-device Apple Intelligence: on iPhones that have Apple Intelligence turned on, Sortd may use Apple's on-device model to read the text of a receipt from a sender it doesn't have a built-in rule for. This runs on your iPhone.
  • Your Google sign-in key: the key that lets Sortd keep reading your receipts (a Google refresh token) is kept in the iPhone Keychain, not in the app's files. Short-lived access keys are only kept in memory while the app runs.
  • No other use: Gmail data is used only to add your purchases to Sortd. It is not used for ads, not sold, not shared, and not read by any person at Sortd.
  • No AI training: Gmail data is never used to develop, improve or train AI or machine learning models, by Sortd or anyone else. Apple's on-device model only reads a receipt to find the purchase, on your iPhone, and learns nothing from it.

Sortd's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. This includes information received from Google Workspace APIs such as the Gmail API: its use will adhere to the Google Workspace API User Data and Developer Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Disconnecting: in Sortd, go to Settings › Purchase Sources, find the account under Email Receipts, and tap Disconnect. Sortd asks Google to cancel its access and deletes the key from the Keychain. You can also choose to delete the purchases that only that Gmail account added. You can also remove Sortd's access from your Google Account's security settings at any time.

Exchange rates

To convert purchases in other currencies, Sortd downloads daily exchange rates from frankfurter.dev, a free public service. The request contains only currency codes and a start date — nothing about you or your purchases. Like any website, that service can see your device's internet address when the request is made.

Notifications

Sortd only sends notifications if you allow them when iOS asks. All of them are scheduled by the app on your iPhone. Sortd doesn't use a push server, so no notification comes from us or passes through us.

  • Check-in (free): if you pick one during setup, Sortd sends a short repeating note each morning, each evening, or on Sunday evening, inviting you to take a look. It is written ahead of time, so it never contains amounts, shops or cards. Change or turn it off in Settings › Bills & Reminders › Check-in.
  • Bill reminders (Sortd Pro): if you turn them on, Sortd reminds you at 9 am the day before a repeat payment is expected. These show the shop, the amount and the card's name.
  • Category limit alerts (Sortd Pro): if bill reminders are on and you have set category limits, Sortd tells you when a category nears or passes its limit, with the amounts.

Notifications with amounts can appear on your Lock Screen. To hide their contents there, go to your iPhone's Settings › Notifications › Sortd › Show Previews. You can turn any of them off in Sortd or in your iPhone's Settings at any time.

Widgets and the setup checklist

Sortd's Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets show numbers from the summary described above. It stays on your iPhone. On the Lock Screen and in StandBy, widgets hide amounts unless you turn on Settings › Privacy & Security › Show Amounts When Locked.

The "Finish setup" list on Home checks whether you have added a Sortd widget by asking iOS on your iPhone. Nothing is sent anywhere.

App lock

If you turn on the lock in Settings › Privacy & Security, iOS checks your Face ID, Touch ID or passcode. Sortd only learns whether the check passed. It never sees your face data, fingerprint or passcode.

Backups and spreadsheets

In Settings › Backup & Data you can save a backup file (your purchases, cards and some settings) or export your purchases as a spreadsheet (CSV). Sortd only makes these when you tap, and hands them to Apple's share sheet. You choose where they go, for example Files, iCloud Drive or AirDrop. Sortd doesn't upload them anywhere.

These files are not locked with a password, so anyone who has the file can read it. Keep them somewhere safe. Sortd deletes its own temporary copy afterwards.

Buying Sortd Pro

Sortd Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment. Sortd never sees your card or your Apple Account details. On your iPhone, Sortd only checks with the App Store whether you have Pro.

Services Sortd talks to

Sortd only connects to:

  • Google (sign-in and the Gmail API) — only if you connect Gmail.
  • frankfurter.dev — for exchange rates, as described above.
  • Apple's App Store — to show prices and check your Sortd Pro purchase.

Apple Intelligence, text reading, widgets, notifications and the app lock all run on your iPhone and don't connect to anything. Sortd has no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking tools built in.

If you email us

Sortd itself never sends email. If you write to support@sortd.page, we get whatever you put in your email, and your email address. Settings › Help & Feedback › Send Feedback opens your own Mail app with your app version, iOS version and iPhone model filled in. You see the email before you send it, and nothing is sent unless you send it.

Email to that address is passed on by Cloudflare, the company that runs this website, to our inbox, which is run by Google. We use it only to reply to you. Ask us and we'll delete it.

How long Sortd keeps data

Everything below is kept on your iPhone only. We hold no copy, so there is nothing for us to keep or delete on our side.

  • Email text: not kept. It is held in memory only while Sortd reads the purchase from it, then discarded straight away.
  • Refunds waiting for a match: if a refund email arrives before Sortd has found its purchase, Sortd keeps the refund's details (shop, amount, currency, date and card's last 4 digits, not the email) for up to 30 days so it can match it later. The next sync after 30 days drops them.
  • Purchases and each email's ID: kept until you delete them, disconnect that Gmail account and choose to delete its purchases, use Delete All Data, or delete the app.
  • Google sign-in key: kept in the Keychain until you disconnect Gmail or use Delete All Data. Sortd then asks Google to cancel it and deletes it. If you are offline, Sortd tries again the next time you open the app.
  • Exchange rates, cards, settings and your setup answers: kept until you change them, use Delete All Data, or delete the app.
  • Scheduled notifications: kept until you turn them off, use Delete All Data, or delete the app.
  • Typed lines, receipt photos and imported statements: not kept. Sortd keeps only the purchases you save.

Your controls

  • Export: Settings › Backup & Data › Export as Spreadsheet gives you every purchase as a CSV file.
  • Change your setup answers: Settings › Help & Feedback › Run Setup Again. The check-in alone: Settings › Bills & Reminders.
  • Delete: Settings › Backup & Data › Delete All Data removes every purchase, card, budget, setting and setup answer from your iPhone, disconnects any Gmail accounts (and asks Google to cancel access), and removes your check-in and any other scheduled notifications. This can't be undone. Backup or spreadsheet files you saved elsewhere are yours, and you delete those yourself.
  • Delete the app: deleting Sortd from your iPhone removes the data in its storage. If you connected Gmail, disconnect it first (or use Delete All Data), or remove Sortd from your Google Account's security settings, so Google's access is cancelled too.

Because we don't hold any of your data, we can't see, change or delete it for you. You are in control of it on your iPhone.

Children

Sortd is a budgeting tool for adults and isn't aimed at children. It doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

Beta sign-ups

This is about the sign-up form on this website, not the app.

  • What we get: your email address, first name, country and whether you use Apple Pay, plus your Gmail address if you give it. We also see which country your internet connection seems to come from, which Cloudflare works out for us.
  • How it reaches us: as one email, sent through Cloudflare, the company that runs this website. It isn't saved in a database, on this website or anywhere else.
  • What we use it for: sending you the TestFlight link when the beta opens. If you gave a Gmail address, we also add it to Google's tester list so Gmail receipts work for you. Nothing else. No newsletters, no marketing.
  • Stopping spam: to stop bots flooding the form, Cloudflare briefly counts sign-ups from each internet connection. That count isn't kept.
  • "I'm human" check: the form uses Cloudflare Turnstile to tell people from bots. To do that, Cloudflare sees details like your internet address and browser type. It sets no cookies. See Cloudflare's Turnstile privacy policy.
  • Who else sees it: no one. We don't share it or sell it.
  • Changed your mind? Email support@sortd.page and we'll delete your sign-up.

Questions or complaints about privacy, or a request about a beta sign-up or an email you sent us: support@sortd.page. We'll reply as soon as we can.
