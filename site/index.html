Why I made Sortd

I split my time between Melbourne and Singapore, which means two currencies, several cards, and absolutely no idea where my money went each month.

Subscriptions were the worst. A few dollars here, a few dollars there, from companies I'd half forgotten about, spread across different cards, accounts and app stores. When I finally added them up, I was paying for more than I thought. Some of it I'd stopped using months ago. (Looking at you, free trial that wasn't.)

So I built Sortd for myself. Every Apple Pay tap and receipt lands in one list without me typing a thing, and every subscription shows up before it renews. I still spend too much on coffee. At least now I know exactly how much.

Questions.

Can't find yours? See Help and Support or email support@sortd.page. A human reads it (hi).

Does Sortd connect to my bank?

No. It never asks for your bank password, and it never will. Purchases come from your Apple Pay taps, your email receipts if you connect Gmail, receipt photos, or what you type in yourself. (Other apps want the keys to your bank. We'd rather not.)

Which purchases are logged automatically?

In-store Apple Pay taps with your iPhone or Apple Watch. Online and in-app purchases come in through email receipts if you connect Gmail.

How does it find my subscriptions?

It looks for payments that repeat, like Netflix every month or a phone bill every four weeks, and reads renewal dates from subscription receipts. Then it shows what's due and what they cost you a month.

Why the Shortcuts app?

Because in most countries Apple doesn't let apps read Wallet directly. We asked. (We didn't ask.) An automation in Shortcuts is Apple's own way to pass each tap to an app, on your phone, with your permission. You set it up once and forget it exists.

Why not Android?

Short version: Sortd hears about your taps through Apple's Shortcuts app, and Android doesn't have one. Long version: see short version. If enough of you ask, we'll build it. (Asking nicely helps.)

Does it work when I travel?

Yes. Purchases are saved in the local currency and converted to your home currency at that day's European Central Bank rate.

What if I have two cards from the same bank?

Add the last 4 digits of each card and its Apple Pay number. That's how Sortd tells them apart.

How much does it cost?

US$6.99 a month, US$49.99 a year, or US$99.99 once. At launch the first year is US$29.99, with a 14-day free trial before that. Every plan has every feature, and the App Store shows the price in your currency.

Which iPhones does it work on?

Any iPhone on iOS 26 or later. Reading receipts with on-device AI needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence. Other iPhones use simpler rules, which work fine for most receipts.
