App Privacy

Sortd's privacy label on the App Store

Data Not Used to Track You Linked to you: usage data, identifiers and diagnostics, so we can see what gets used and fix crashes. Never your purchases.

Stored on your iPhone

Your purchases, cards and settings stay on your phone. Turn on iCloud backup and an encrypted copy goes to your own iCloud. We can't read either.

No bank login

Sortd never asks for your bank password. It only keeps the last 4 digits of a card, to know which card you used.

No email access

Sortd doesn't read your email and never asks to. Your inbox stays your problem. Receipts come from the camera, and from statements you pick.

Counts, not coffees

Sortd counts which features get used, never what you spend. No ads, no “partners”. Turn it off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Privacy. Nobody is watching you buy coffee.

No account needed

Sign in with Apple or Google only if you want to. Google only tells Sortd who you are. Export every purchase, or delete everything, in Settings › Backup & Data.

Why Sortd exists

Most people have no idea where their money went last month. Not because they're bad with money. Because it's spread across several cards, a couple of bank apps, three app stores and a drawer full of receipts.

Subscriptions are the worst of it. A few dollars here, a few dollars there, from companies you half forgot about. Add them up and it's usually more than you thought, including a few you stopped using months ago. (Looking at you, free trial that wasn't.)

So we built the app we wanted: Apple Pay purchases land in one list without typing a thing, paper receipts take one photo, and every subscription shows up before it renews. You'll still spend too much on coffee. At least now you'll know exactly how much.

Questions.

Can't find yours? See Help and Support or email support@sortd.page. A human reads it (hi).

Does Sortd connect to my bank?

No. It never asks for your bank password, and it never will. Purchases come from your Apple Pay payments, receipt photos, bank statements you import, or what you type in yourself. (Other apps want the keys to your bank. We'd rather not.)

What's the catch?

There isn't one. Every feature is free, there are no ads, and your purchases stay on your phone, so there's nothing to sell. (We know. We were suspicious too.)

So how do you make money?

We sell your data. (Kidding. We can't. It's on your phone.) Honestly, we mostly don't. Sortd costs almost nothing to run, because your purchases never touch our servers.

Which purchases are logged automatically?

Apple Pay in shops, in apps and on websites. Taps in shops are logged from the tap. Payments in apps and on websites are logged from Wallet's notification, on iOS 27 or later, if your card sends one. Apple's trigger can miss one now and then. (It's theirs. We've asked nicely.) Cash and cards that aren't in Apple Pay never show up by themselves, so add those by hand, with the camera or from a statement.

How does it find my subscriptions?

It looks for payments that repeat, like Netflix every month or a phone bill every four weeks, and works out when the next one is due. Then it shows what's due and what they cost you a month. Most people find one they'd forgotten about. Some find four.

Why the Shortcuts app?

Because in most countries Apple doesn't let apps read Wallet directly. We asked. (We didn't ask.) An automation in Shortcuts is Apple's own way to pass each tap to an app, on your phone, with your permission. You set it up once and forget it exists.

Why not Android?

Short version: Sortd hears about your taps through Apple's Shortcuts app, and Android doesn't have one. Long version: see short version. If enough of you ask, we'll build it. (Asking nicely helps.)

Does it work when I travel?

Yes. Purchases are saved in the local currency and converted to your home currency at that day's European Central Bank rate. The holiday is still expensive. Now it's expensive and itemised.

What if I have two cards from the same bank?

Add the last 4 digits of each card and its Apple Pay number. That's how Sortd tells them apart.

Which iPhones does it work on?

Any iPhone on iOS 26 or later. Logging Apple Pay in apps and on websites needs iOS 27. Reading receipts with on‑device AI needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence. Other iPhones use simpler rules, which work fine for most receipts.
